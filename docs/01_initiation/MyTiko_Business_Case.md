# Business Case

## 1\. Executive Summary

Kenya's live events industry generates an estimated KES 50 billion or more annually across concerts, sports, corporate conferences, cultural festivals, weddings, and community gatherings. Yet the infrastructure supporting this economy remains fragmented, fraud-prone, and financially opaque. Event organizers rely on patchwork systems - WhatsApp broadcast lists, M-Pesa-to-personal-number payments, Google Forms, and manual spreadsheets - that produce reconciliation nightmares, zero data visibility, rampant ticket counterfeiting, and regulatory blind spots under an increasingly digital compliance regime.

MYTIKO is a secure, scalable, and transparent event ticketing and financial infrastructure platform purpose-built for this market. It eliminates ticket fraud through dynamic time-rotating QR codes and device fingerprinting, ensures zero oversell via real-time inventory locking, provides complete financial transparency through a double-entry ledger with split-fee recording, and maintains full regulatory compliance - including KRA Etims fiscal receipting, the Kenya Data Protection Act 2019, CBK payment regulations, and PCI-DSS - from the very first transaction. Beyond core ticketing, MYTIKO introduces two market-defining capabilities at launch: Lipa Pole Pole (instalment payments for high-value tickets, backed by M-Pesa) and an Influencer Marketing & Commission Engine that allows organizers to run real-time, performance-tracked promotional campaigns through a network of social media influencers and community ambassadors.

The platform serves every scale of event host - from an individual running a 30-person chama fundraiser (Starter tier, zero monthly fee, 3.5% per transaction) to a corporation hosting a 50,000-person stadium event (Enterprise tier, custom pricing, dedicated infrastructure, SLA contract). Phase 1 is structured as a three-stage rollout: Stage 1 delivers the core platform with payments, compliance, Lipa Pole Pole, and the Influencer Engine; Stage 2 adds native mobile apps, ticket transfer, flash-sale infrastructure, and mobile wallet integration; Stage 3 brings group booking discounts, AI-powered recommendations, and white-label corporate branding. This staged approach delivers market value early while building toward a complete ecosystem within 18 months.

## 3\. The MYTIKO Solution

MYTIKO addresses every pain point identified above through a comprehensive platform delivered in three stages within Phase 1. The architecture follows a clean-layered design with 12 bounded-context services (Auth, Event, Ticket, Payment, Notification, Validation, Geo, Compliance, Fraud, Analytics, AI, and Admin), each maintaining strict separation of domain logic, application orchestration, infrastructure adapters, and API endpoints. The MVP launches as a modular monolith for deployment simplicity, with microservice extraction planned as traffic and team scaling demand.

### 3.1 Stage 1 - Core Platform (Months 1-8)

Stage 1 delivers the minimum set of features required to legally and safely process live paid ticketing transactions in Kenya, combined with two differentiating capabilities that establish MYTIKO's market position from Day 1.

Event management: A guided 5-step creation wizard enables hosts of any technical skill level to publish a live event in under 10 minutes. The system supports up to 20 ticket tiers per event (VIP, Early Bird, General Admission, Group, Student, Complimentary, and Custom), with early bird auto-expiry (transitions to next tier at a configurable date or sales threshold), independent capacity limits per tier enforced by the system rather than honor-based, bundle pricing (buy 4 get 1 free with configurable threshold), and per-order quantity limits (minimum 1, maximum 10) to prevent scalping. Events follow a formal lifecycle state machine: DRAFT → REVIEW → PUBLISHED → \[SOLD_OUT | ON_SALE | PAUSED\] → COMPLETED / CANCELLED.

Dynamic TOTP QR codes: Ticket QR codes are generated using the Time-based One-Time Password algorithm (RFC 6238), tied to the ticket ID and a per-event cryptographic secret. The QR image refreshes every 15 to 60 seconds (configurable by the organiser), meaning a screenshot becomes invalid after a single refresh interval. The attendee app pre-generates the next three QR codes offline, ensuring display works without internet. Each QR is accompanied by a 6-digit alphanumeric backup code for manual entry at the gate. Gate validation checks four conditions simultaneously: ticket ID is valid, time window is correct, ticket has not already been used, and device binding matches.

Payment infrastructure with per-rail policies: M-Pesa STK Push via the Safaricom Daraja API is the primary payment rail (expected to handle 85% of transactions), supplemented by M-Pesa C2B (PayBill/Till) for manual payments, Pesapal for Visa, Mastercard, and Airtel Money, and a cash door-sales mode tracked as CASH_SALE. Each payment rail has a precisely defined payment window and grace period enforced by a dedicated domain policy: 5 minutes for M-Pesa STK (matching Safaricom's native STK expiry), 30 minutes for PayBill, 15 minutes plus a 2-minute grace for card payments (accommodating 3DS2 redirect latency), 10 minutes for Apple Pay and Google Pay, 30 minutes for PayPal, and 24 hours plus a 1-hour grace for bank EFT via Pesalink. A background job runs every 60 seconds to expire unpaid orders, process them in batches of 100 with controlled parallelism, and release inventory back to the pool. Late payment callbacks (e.g., a Daraja confirmation arriving after order expiry) are tolerated within a 30-second grace window; otherwise, automatic reversal is initiated with three retries before escalation to a manual refund queue. Anti-abuse rules limit any user to a maximum of 2 concurrent pending orders across all events and 1 pending order per event.

Double-entry financial ledger: No balance is stored as a single mutable number anywhere in the system. All balances are derived by summing credits and debits across four account types: Escrow (credited on payment, debited on payout or refund), Revenue (credited on platform fee earned, debited on fee refund), Payable (credited on event completion, debited on payout disbursement), and Refund (debited on refund issuance). Every transaction records face value, platform service fee, gateway processing fee, and VAT as separate line items. Tax rates (VAT at 16%, SEP Tax, Withholding Tax) are frozen at the moment of transaction so that future rate changes never retroactively alter historic records. This produces the complete, tamper-evident audit trail required by KRA and internal auditors.

Lipa Pole Pole (instalment payments): For events with tickets priced above a configurable threshold (default KES 3,000), attendees may purchase through an instalment plan. The organizer configures the number of instalments (2 to 6), the deposit percentage (minimum 30%), and the payment schedule (weekly, bi-weekly, or monthly). The system enforces a minimum of 14 days between purchase and event start to allow all instalments to complete. Each instalment triggers an M-Pesa STK Push on the scheduled date. If an instalment fails or is missed, the system sends three escalating reminders (SMS + push notification) over 48 hours. If payment is not completed after the grace window, the ticket reservation is released and all previously paid amounts are refunded to the attendee's M-Pesa, minus a configurable administrative fee (default 5% of amount paid, capped at KES 500). The organizer dashboard displays a dedicated Lipa Pole Pole tracking view showing paid, pending, overdue, and defaulted instalment plans. This feature directly addresses the affordability barrier that prevents attendees from purchasing high-value tickets and is a significant market differentiator with no equivalent on any competing Kenyan ticketing platform.

Influencer Marketing & Commission Engine: MYTIKO introduces a first-of-its-kind structured influencer marketing system for the Kenyan events industry. The feature works as follows. An organizer creates a marketing campaign for their event, specifying the total number of tickets allocated for influencer-driven sales, the commission rate per ticket sold (fixed amount or percentage of face value), and the campaign start and end dates (the campaign ends automatically when the event concludes). The organizer then invites influencers (social media personalities, community ambassadors, brand partners, or any individual) to the campaign. Each invited influencer receives a unique batch code - a short, branded, shareable link and QR code - that they distribute through their channels (Instagram, TikTok, Twitter/X, WhatsApp, YouTube, or any medium). When an attendee purchases a ticket using an influencer's batch code, the sale is attributed to that influencer in real time. A live leaderboard, visible to the organizer and optionally to all participating influencers, ranks every influencer by tickets sold, revenue generated, and conversion rate. The leaderboard updates in real time via WebSocket push until the event concludes. After the event, a comprehensive performance matrix is generated for each influencer, scoring them across multiple dimensions: total tickets sold, revenue generated, average order value, conversion rate (clicks to purchases), cost per acquisition (commission paid divided by tickets sold), and audience quality (refund rate and no-show rate for tickets sold through their code). This matrix produces a recommended action for each influencer: retain (top performers), develop (mid-tier with potential), warn (underperformers who may improve), or offload (consistently low performers or those with high refund/no-show rates). The organizer can archive this data to build a cumulative influencer roster across multiple events, enabling data-driven decisions about whom to engage for future campaigns. Commission payouts to influencers are processed via M-Pesa B2C within the standard payout cycle, subject to the same KYC requirements as organizer payouts. Every commission payment generates a KRA Etims receipt. This system replaces the informal, WhatsApp-based influencer arrangements that currently dominate the Kenyan events industry, where there is no tracking, no commission logic, no performance data, and frequent disputes between organizers and promoters.

Refund engine: Three configurable modes cover all scenarios. Full Goodwill refund returns 100% of face value to the attendee, with the organizer absorbing the gateway fee. Partial Net refund returns face value minus all fees. Platform Absorbs refund returns 100% at MYTIKO's cost (rare, used for platform errors). An automated batch refund is triggered when an event is cancelled, processing all sold tickets in the configured mode. M-Pesa refunds are processed via Daraja B2C within 24 hours; card refunds within 5 to 7 business days. Late M-Pesa callbacks that arrive after order expiry trigger automatic reversal with three retries before escalation.

Organizer payouts: Funds are disbursed on a scheduled basis post-event: T+5 business days for Starter tier, T+3 for Professional, and custom schedule (including T+1) for Enterprise. Disbursement channels include M-Pesa B2C and Pesa link/EFT bank transfer. A KRA iTax compliance check is performed before every payout - non-compliant payouts are blocked until the organizer resolves their tax status.

KRA Etims fiscal receipting: Every ticket sale generates a real-time fiscal invoice submitted to the KRA Etims API within 30 seconds of payment confirmation. KRA returns a Control Unit Invoice Number (CUIN) that is stored on the transaction record and printed on the ticket PDF and confirmation screen. Refunds void the original invoice. MYTIKO is registered as the DSN (Device Serial Number) holder and issues all invoices on behalf of organizers - organizers do not need their own ETR device. If the Etims API fails, the system retries three times at 5-minute intervals, then flags the item in the admin compliance dashboard. Ticket delivery is never blocked by an Etims failure.

Kenya Data Protection Act compliance: A three-tier KYC system is enforced. Tier 1 (all users): phone number verification via OTP (6 digits, 10-minute TTL via Africa's Talking), with name and email optional at this stage. ODPC consent is recorded with timestamp, IP address, and consent text version. Tier 2 (organizers and payout recipients): KRA PIN verification via iTax API, business registration number verification via eCitizen BRS API, and bank account or M-Pesa number for payouts. Tier 3 (corporate and enterprise): director details and ID verification via IPRS API, AML sanctions screening, Data Processing Agreement signature, and manual review by the MYTIKO compliance team within 2 business days. Data subject rights are supported with defined SLAs: access requests within 72 hours, rectification within 48 hours, erasure within 72 hours (PII zeroed; KRA transaction records retained for 7 years per tax law), portability within 72 hours, and objection to processing triggers immediate account freeze with review within 5 days.

Gate scanning: The gate scanner is built as a Web Assembly progressive web app with a guaranteed 8-hour offline operating capability. Before the event, the scanner syncs an AES-256 encrypted ticket manifest to local storage. Scan-to-result time targets are under 800 milliseconds when online and under 200 milliseconds when offline. The scanner displays clear colour-coded results: green for valid, orange for already scanned, and red for invalid. A spike in invalid scans at any gate triggers an automatic fraud alert to the organizer dashboard in real time. Offline scan data syncs back to the server when connectivity resumes.

Real-time dashboards: The organizer dashboard is delivered as a server-rendered web application with WebSocket-driven live updates covering revenue overview (gross, net, escrow, payable), sales velocity (tickets per minute as a live sparkline), per-tier breakdown, payment method split, refund status, payout status, Lipa Pole Pole instalment tracking, and influencer campaign leaderboard. The gate check-in dashboard shows scans per minute, admitted versus capacity, per-gate throughput, invalid scan alerts, and a no-show projection. An automated post-event PDF report is generated within 2 hours of event completion and emailed to the organizer. The admin console provides KYC approval workflows, Etims monitoring, payout override, fraud flags, audit log (every admin action logged with UserID, IP, timestamp, and before/after state), dead letter queue management, and ODPC data subject request fulfilment.

Notifications: SMS via Africa's Talking with a branded "MYTIKO" sender ID (registered with the Communications Authority of Kenya), email via SendGrid (with ticket PDF attachment and calendar .ics file), and push notifications via FCM (Android) and APNs (iOS). Channels are used for payment confirmation, ticket transfer notifications, refund processing, 24-hour event reminders, waitlist spot availability, and Lipa Pole Pole instalment reminders.

### 3.2 Stage 2 - Platform Expansion (Months 8-12)

Stage 2 extends the platform's reach and user experience after the core is proven and stable in production.

Native Android and iOS applications: While the Stage 1 progressive web app covers all core flows, native apps (Kotlin for Android, Swift for iOS) deliver superior performance, deeper OS integration, and access to platform-specific capabilities. The Android app targets the 85%+ of Kenyan smartphone users on Android, while the iOS app captures the growing premium segment. Both apps share the same backend API but offer native navigation, biometric authentication (fingerprint and Face ID), background push notifications, and deep-link support for influencer batch codes.

Ticket transfer and gifting: Ticket holders can transfer a ticket to another registered MYTIKO user - a single transfer per ticket. The transfer locks out the original holder's QR code and activates a new QR code for the recipient. Gift tickets allow purchasing on behalf of another phone number, with the recipient receiving an onboarding prompt. A complete transfer audit trail is maintained for gate dispute resolution. Organizers can optionally disable transfers for specific events or limit the number of transfers per ticket (default maximum of 2).

Waiting room and virtual queue for flash sales: For high-demand events, organizers can enable a pre-queue that activates before the sale opens. Attendees joining early receive a queue position via WebSocket. The system releases users to checkout in controlled batches (configurable: 100 to 1,000 per minute) to prevent server overload. Queue position is shown as a live progress bar with no manual refresh required. Fair FIFO ordering is enforced by a sorted set with timestamp as score. An anti-bot challenge is presented before queue entry.

Apple Wallet and Google Wallet integration: Ticket holders can add their ticket to Apple Wallet (PKPass) or Google Wallet with a single tap. The wallet pass includes the event name, date, time, venue, seat/tier information, and a dynamically updating QR code. Wallet passes update automatically when a ticket is transferred or cancelled. This provides instant offline access to the ticket without opening the MYTIKO app.

### 3.3 Stage 3 - Market Differentiation (Months 12-18)

Stage 3 adds capabilities that deepen monetization, enhance discoverability, and unlock the enterprise market.

Group booking discounts: Organizers can configure automatic percentage discounts when the order quantity meets or exceeds a threshold (e.g., 10% off when buying 5 or more tickets). The discount logic is per-tier and per-event, with the ability to set minimum and maximum group sizes. Group discounts are visible to buyers at checkout and reflected in the financial ledger as a separate discount line item.

AI-powered event recommendations (Gemini): An AI layer, powered by Google's Gemini model, delivers personalized event recommendations based on attendee purchase history, location, preferences, and trending events. For organizers, Gemini provides smart event description generation (the organizer provides bullet points, the AI writes compelling copy), predictive sales forecasting based on historical data and market signals, dynamic pricing suggestions (recommending price adjustments based on sales velocity and remaining inventory), and attendee sentiment analysis from post-event feedback. The AI service operates as a stateless service that can be enabled or disabled without affecting core platform operations.

White-label and corporate branding: Enterprise-tier clients receive a fully branded ticketing experience where all attendee-facing surfaces display the client's brand with no MYTIKO branding visible. This includes a custom domain (e.g., events.clientbrand.co.ke), branded email templates, branded ticket PDFs, and a branded checkout flow. The white-label configuration is stored per-organization and applied at the gateway level, requiring no code changes for each new enterprise client. This is a key revenue driver for the Enterprise tier, where custom pricing and SLA contracts command premium margins.

### 6.2 Unit Economics

Starter tier example: A 200-person event at KES 1,500 per ticket generates gross sales of KES 300,000. Platform fee at 3.5% yields KES 10,500. Gateway fee (M-Pesa effective rate approximately 1.5%) costs KES 4,500. Organizer receives a net payout of KES 285,000. MYTIKO gross revenue is KES 10,500 with COGS (infrastructure and payment processing) of approximately KES 2,500, producing a gross margin of approximately 76% on this single event.

Professional tier example: An organizer running 4 events per month averaging 1,000 tickets at KES 2,500 generates monthly gross sales of KES 10,000,000. Platform fee at 2.0% yields KES 200,000 per month, plus the KES 5,000 subscription, totaling KES 205,000 per month in MYTIKO revenue. Monthly COGS of approximately KES 45,000 yield a gross margin of approximately 78%.

Influencer marketing revenue uplift: An event with 10 active influencers, each driving an average of 50 ticket sales (500 total incremental tickets at KES 2,000), generates KES 1,000,000 in incremental GMV. At a 3.5% platform fee, this adds KES 35,000 in platform revenue per event. If MYTIKO charges a 1% campaign management fee on top, an additional KES 10,000 is earned, bringing total influencer-attributed revenue to KES 45,000 per event with near-zero incremental COGS.

## 9\. Recommendation

Based on the scale of the market opportunity (KES 100M+ annual events industry), the absence of any regulatory-compliant, financially transparent, influencer-enabled digital ticketing platform in Kenya, the strong unit economics (76-78% gross margins with break-even projected at Month 8), and the competitive differentiation provided by Lipa Pole Pole and the Influencer Marketing Engine, it is recommended that MYTIKO proceed to full project authorization under the three-stage Phase 1 structure described in this document. The staged approach mitigates execution risk by delivering market value incrementally while building toward a complete ecosystem within 18 months.

**PART C**

# Feasibility Study

## 1\. Purpose

This study evaluates whether MYTIKO's three-stage Phase 1 roadmap - including the expanded scope of Lipa Pole Pole, the Influencer Marketing Engine, native mobile apps, AI recommendations, and white-label branding - can be successfully delivered within the proposed 18-month timeline and KES 70M budget.

## 2\. Technical Feasibility

### 2.1 Architecture

The platform is designed as 12 bounded-context services following a clean-layered architecture where the domain layer contains all business rules with zero infrastructure dependencies, the application layer orchestrates commands, queries, and event handling, the infrastructure layer implements persistence, payment gateways, messaging, and background jobs, and the API layer exposes endpoints. A shared kernel provides foundational primitives (entities, aggregate roots, value objects, a Result monad for error handling without exceptions), cross-cutting pipeline behaviors (validation, logging, idempotency, transaction management), and shared contracts for inter-service communication.

Stage 1 deploys as a modular monolith - all services in a single deployable unit with logical separation. This minimizes deployment complexity, simplifies debugging, and enables single-database transactions across service boundaries. As traffic grows through Stages 2 and 3, individual high-load services (Payment, Ticket, Validation) can be extracted into independently deployable units without architectural changes, since the clean-layer boundaries are already enforced.

### 2.3 Lipa Pole Pole Technical Assessment

The instalment payment feature adds a scheduling dimension to the payment domain. Each Lipa Pole Pole plan is modelled as an aggregate containing an ordered collection of instalment records, each with a scheduled date, amount, and status (pending, paid, overdue, failed, refunded). A recurring background job evaluates due instalments daily and triggers M-Pesa STK Push payments on schedule. The key technical challenge is handling partial-payment states: if a buyer has paid 3 of 5 instalments and defaults, the system must correctly refund the paid amount minus the administrative fee and release the ticket back to inventory. This requires careful coordination between the Payment, Ticket, and Notification services, managed through domain events and the transactional outbox. The approach is technically sound and follows the same patterns already established in the core payment pipeline.

### 2.4 Influencer Engine Technical Assessment

The Influencer Marketing Engine introduces three new components: a Campaign aggregate (with batch code generation, influencer invitations, and commission configuration), a real-time leaderboard (Redis sorted set updated on every ticket purchase attributed to a batch code, with WebSocket push to all subscribers), and a performance matrix calculation (a scheduled post-event job that computes multi-dimensional scores and generates recommendations). Batch code attribution is deterministic - the code is embedded in the purchase URL or entered at checkout, creating an immutable link between the sale and the influencer. Commission payouts follow the same B2C disbursement pipeline as organizer payouts, ensuring KRA Etims receipt generation and KYC compliance. The leaderboard uses Redis sorted sets, which provide O (log N) insert and rank operations, supporting hundreds of influencers per campaign with sub-100 millisecond update latency. This is a well-understood data structure with no novel technical risk.

### 2.5 Native App Technical Assessment (Stage 2)

The native Android (Kotlin) and iOS (Swift) apps consume the same backend API as the PWA, ensuring feature parity without backend duplication. The primary technical investment is in platform-specific UI implementation, biometric authentication integration (fingerprint and Face ID), deep-link handling for influencer batch codes, and Apple Wallet / Google Wallet pass generation. The wallet pass generation uses established standards (PKPass for Apple, Google Wallet API for Android) with well-documented libraries. The risk is moderate and primarily related to App Store review timelines, which are outside MYTIKO's control.

### 2.6 AI Integration Technical Assessment (Stage 3)

The AI layer is designed as a stateless service that can be enabled, disabled, or replaced without affecting any other platform functionality. Gemini API calls are made from a Python FastAPI microservice that sits alongside the .NET monolith but communicates via HTTP. Recommendation models require a minimum dataset (estimated 10,000 ticket purchases across 200+ events) to produce meaningful personalization, which is why this feature is placed in Stage 3 rather than earlier. Smart description generation and predictive forecasting can operate with less data and may be activated earlier if data volume permits. The primary risk is Gemini API pricing changes, mitigated by designing the AI service as a pluggable adapter that can swap to alternative models.

## 3\. Financial Feasibility

The expanded Phase 1 scope increases the budget from approximately KES 40M (for a minimal MVP) to KES 70M to cover native mobile development, influencer network seeding, Gemini API costs, and a larger engineering team. The 18-month runway provides sufficient time for all three stages, with break-even still projected at Month 8 (after Stage 1 GA). The incremental investment in Stages 2 and 3 is justified by the revenue uplift from influencer campaign fees, the Enterprise tier pricing enabled by white-label branding, and the higher engagement and retention driven by native apps and AI recommendations.

The capital structure can be equity (founder, angel, or seed VC), grant (Kenya Startup Act 2022 incentives for qualifying startups), or a blended approach. Comparable raises in the Kenyan startup ecosystem suggest that KES 70M (approximately USD 540,000) is achievable for a team with domain expertise, a detailed product specification, and a working Stage 1 prototype.

## 4\. Operational Feasibility

The core team requires 10 to 15 people across engineering, design, product, compliance, marketing, and operations. The phased hiring plan matches the staged rollout: Stage 1 focuses on backend, frontend (PWA), and compliance hires; Stage 2 adds mobile engineers (Kotlin, Swift); Stage 3 adds a data/AI engineer and enterprise sales. A dedicated Organizer Success team of 3 to 4 people handles onboarding, and a separate Influencer Relations function (1 to 2 people) manages the ambassador network, recruitment, and dispute resolution. Nairobi has a growing pool of experienced software engineers, particularly in fintech and mobile development, with competitive senior salaries ranging from KES 300,000 to 600,000 per month.

## 5\. Market Feasibility

Kenya's smartphone penetration exceeds 60% and continues growing. M-Pesa processes over KES 30 trillion annually, with users comfortable making digital payments. Post-COVID, live events have rebounded strongly with a new expectation for contactless, digital ticketing. KRA's Etims mandate is pushing all businesses toward digital invoicing. The influencer marketing opportunity is particularly compelling: Kenya has one of Africa's most active social media ecosystems, with influencers already driving ticket sales informally through WhatsApp and Instagram - MYTIKO formalizes and monetizes this existing behavior.

Go-to-market is staged to match the product rollout. Stage 1 targets Nairobi with 10 anchor organizers, seeding the influencer network with 50 ambassadors across music, comedy, sports, and corporate event niches. Stage 2 expands to Mombasa and Kisumu alongside the native app launch, leveraging App Store visibility for organic growth. Stage 3 activates the Enterprise pipeline with white-label proof-of-concept deployments for 3 corporate clients.

## 7\. Schedule Feasibility

The 18-month three-stage timeline is achievable given the clear scope boundaries between stages and the Go/No-Go gates that prevent premature advancement. Stage 1 (8 months to GA) is the most technically dense phase, but the payment domain - the most complex component - has already been specified at a production-code level. The Influencer Engine, while a new feature, follows established patterns (batch code attribution, Redis sorted sets for leaderboards, and the existing payout pipeline for commission disbursement). Stage 2 (4 months) focuses on native app development, which is parallelizable with Stage 1 stabilization. Stage 3 (6 months) adds AI and white-label, both of which are architecturally isolated and can be developed with minimal coupling to the core platform.

The critical path for Stage 1 runs through Daraja sandbox access (2 to 4 weeks), KRA Etims DSN registration, and penetration testing. For Stage 2, the critical path is App Store review timelines. For Stage 3, it is Gemini API integration and enterprise sales pipeline maturity.

MYTIKO's three-stage Phase 1 is feasible across all six evaluated dimensions, with an average confidence of 82%. The expanded scope introduces two significant market differentiators (Lipa Pole Pole and the Influencer Marketing Engine) that no competing Kenyan platform offers, while the staged rollout mitigates execution risk by delivering value incrementally and gating each stage on the success of the previous one.

The incremental investment (KES 70M versus approximately KES 40M for a minimal MVP) is justified by the substantially larger addressable market: instalment payments unlock the price-sensitive segment, influencer marketing drives organic acquisition and positions MYTIKO as an ecosystem platform rather than a transactional tool, native apps capture the 60%+ of users who prefer native experience, and white-label branding unlocks the high-value Enterprise tier. The three-stage approach means MYTIKO can begin generating revenue and validating market fit from Month 8, well before the full investment is deployed.

## Appendix B: Glossary

| DSN | Device Serial Number - KRA Etims registration identifier; MYTIKO holds the DSN on behalf of all organizers |
| ODPC | Office of the Data Protection Commissioner - Kenya's data protection regulator |
| DSAR | Data Subject Access Request - individual's right to access their personal data |

| PCI-DSS | Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard |
| PKPass | Apple Wallet pass format for mobile tickets |
| GMV | Gross Merchandise Value - total value of tickets sold through the platform |
| PWA | Progressive Web App - web application with native app-like capabilities including offline support |
| WASM | WebAssembly - binary format enabling near-native performance in the browser |
| Clean Architecture | Onion architecture with Domain at center; dependencies point inward only; infrastructure is outermost |
| B2C | Business-to-Consumer - M-Pesa disbursement from MYTIKO's business account to individual mobile wallets |
